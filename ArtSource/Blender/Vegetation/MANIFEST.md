# Veyra City vegetation set - MANIFEST

Original tree, hedge and shrub set authored for Veyra City. Replaces the placeholder Perlin-sphere
tree meshes. Nothing here is derived from another game's assets: the benchmark frames in
`ReferenceImages/08_GTAV_VideoCapture_2026-11-12/` were read for silhouette, canopy density and
trunk-to-canopy ratio only, and every mesh and texel in this folder is generated by the scripts in
`scripts/`.

Built with Blender 5.2.3 LTS, headless. Exported through `Tools/Blender/vc_tools.py`
(`VC_Unity_Prop`), 1 Blender unit = 1 m.

## Material count: 2, for the entire vegetation set

| Material | Textures (2048 / 1024, power of two) | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| `M_Veyra_Foliage` | `T_Veyra_Foliage_Atlas_{BaseColor,Normal,MetallicSmoothness}.png` (2048) | **alpha clip**, cutoff 0.5, **Render Face: Both** |
| `M_Veyra_Bark` | `T_Veyra_Bark_Atlas_{BaseColor,Normal,MetallicSmoothness}.png` (1024) | opaque |

Every species shares ONE foliage atlas rather than one atlas per family. Each mesh therefore has
exactly 2 submeshes, and the whole vegetation set is 2 materials - with GPU instancing, a street
of palms, hedges and shrubs batches as 2 material states rather than 7+. This is a deliberate
response to BUG-170 (open P1 CPU budget failure, main-thread p95 18-93 ms against 10 ms).

`MetallicSmoothness`: metallic 0 in RGB, **smoothness = 1 - roughness in the alpha channel**, per
CLAUDE.md (URP Lit, Smoothness Source = Metallic Alpha). Foliage smoothness is clamped to <= 0.38;
anything glossier gives vegetation a wet-plastic sheen under the Veyra sun.

### Foliage atlas tiles (19)

Clusters: `palm_royal_frond`, `palm_fan_frond`, `palm_skirt`, `broadleaf_a`, `broadleaf_b`, `cypress_sprig`, `pine_spray`, `boxwood`, `scrub_a`, `scrub_b`, `grass_tuft`, `leaf_small`
Billboards (LOD2): `bb_royal`, `bb_fan`, `bb_broadleaf`, `bb_cypress`, `bb_pine`, `bb_shrub`, `bb_hedge`
(`bb_hedge` is unused: the hedge LOD2 is a plain clipped box, which beats a billboard for a
wall-shaped object. It is kept in the atlas as a spare slot.)

## Triangle budget

Brief: LOD0 under 4000 for large trees, under 1500 for hedges and shrubs, LOD2 genuinely tiny.
Every asset is inside budget with large headroom - the heaviest tree is 1552 tris, 38% of its
allowance, and LOD2 is 4-20 tris (2-3 crossed billboard cards; the hedge LOD2 is a plain 6-face
clipped box, which beats a billboard for a wall-shaped object). Total for all 17 LOD0 meshes is
9732 tris, i.e. the entire vegetation library costs less than three of the old blob trees did at
their worst.

Where silhouette and triangles conflicted, triangles won, and in two places that is visible:
- **Palm fronds** are V-folded card strips (7 segments), not modelled leaflets. A frond reads
  correctly from the ground and in profile; from directly above, the fold is obvious.
- **LOD2 billboard aspect**. The billboard card is sized to match the LOD0 crown width so the
  silhouette does not pop at the transition, which stretches the painted tile horizontally for the
  fan palm (~1.3x) and the royal palm (~1.1x). Correct at LOD2 distance (60 m+), wrong if anyone
  forces LOD2 up close.

### The density rule that actually matters

Spacing above is art direction. This is the performance contract:

    instances_in_view  ~=  density * pi * cull_distance^2

Keep that under **6000 per module type**, and under **12000 for all vegetation combined**. At the
triangle counts in the tables above that is roughly 200-350k triangles of vegetation on screen,
which is comfortable on an RTX 4070 and - with GPU instancing on both foliage materials and the
bark material - costs a handful of draw calls rather than thousands.

Worked example: `VC_Ground_GrassPatch_M` at 0.3 /m^2 culled at 45 m gives 0.3 * pi * 45^2 = 1900
instances, 53k triangles. The same density culled at 150 m instead gives 21000 instances. The
density is not what breaks the budget - the cull distance is, because it is squared. If a density
has to rise, lower the cull distance first.

Given BUG-170 (main-thread p95 18-93 ms against a 10 ms budget), the thing to watch on the CPU side
is not triangles at all but the number of distinct renderers submitted per frame. Scatter through
instanced rendering or a batched scatter system, not one GameObject per plant.

## Technique

- **Leaf cards against a packed atlas**, never individual leaf geometry. Each card is one quad
  carrying a painted cluster, frond or sprig.
- **Alpha clip, not blend.** Atlas alpha is hard-cut (>0.5 -> 1, <0.22 -> 0) and colour is dilated
  14 px outward into the transparent region so mips and trilinear filtering never pull black
  fringes around leaves.
- **Canopy-biased normals.** Every leaf card's shading normal is blended 0.70-0.85 toward the
  canopy volume normal (radial from the crown centre, or the lobe centre for broadleaf clumps, or
  the branch-plus-up direction for conifers) and written as custom split normals. The verify pass
  measures the mean angle between each loop normal and its flat polygon normal: 21-54 deg across
  the set, i.e. the bias is really in the exported files and foliage shades as a volume rather than
  as cardboard catching the sun edge-on.
- **Authored LODs, not decimated.** A Decimate collapse on leaf cards shreds the silhouette, so
  LOD1 is rebuilt with fewer cards and coarser trunks (32-41% of LOD0) and LOD2 is 2-3 crossed
  billboard cards.
- **Bark tiling inside an atlas.** Trunk UVs wrap the circumference across one atlas tile in U and
  restart the tile in V every ~2 m of height. The bark tiles are seamless in both axes, so this
  reads as tiling without any UV ever leaving its atlas rect.
- **Hedges** are clipped-box shells of ~0.5 m cells, each cell carrying one full-bleed boxwood tile
  with a random flip, plus ragged `leaf_small` cards along the clipped edges to break the box
  silhouette. The ends are capped flush at the module boundary so straight modules butt together.

## Collision

`UCX_<name>`, convex, **trunk only** - 24-36 tris each. Canopies have no collision, as specified.

Two deliberate deviations, flag if unwanted:
- **Hedges get `UBX_<name>`** (BoxCollider, 12 tris) covering the clipped volume. A hedge has no
  trunk, and a 2.5 m hedge wall that cars drive through would be worse than the rule it breaks.
- **Shrubs get no collider at all** - roadside scrub is meant to be driven over.

## Unity import notes (for whoever lands these in `Assets/`)

1. Import with the project's `VeyraModelImportDefaults` (Scale Factor 1, Convert Units on, Bake
   Axis Conversion on, Read/Write off). Normals: **Import** (do not recalculate - that would throw
   away the canopy-biased custom normals, which is the whole point).
2. Unity builds one LODGroup on the FBX root from the `_LOD0/1/2` suffixes. Suggested screen-relative
   transition heights: trees 0.45 / 0.12 / 0.012, hedges and shrubs 0.40 / 0.10 / 0.015.
3. Create the two materials as serialised assets in `Assets/_Veyra/World/Generated/Materials` and
   register them in `VeyraMaterialLibrary` - runtime code must never `Shader.Find` (CLAUDE.md).
   `M_Veyra_Foliage`: URP/Lit, Surface Opaque, **Alpha Clipping on, Threshold 0.5**, Render Face
   **Both**, Smoothness Source **Metallic Alpha**, normal map marked Normal Map.
4. Textures: BC7 on PC, ASTC 6x6 on Android; BaseColor sRGB, Normal as Normal Map, MetallicSmoothness
   **non-sRGB**.
5. Enable GPU instancing on both materials. These are meant to be instanced in the thousands.
6. The FBX root carries rotation (0, 180, 0), which is expected from `VC_Unity_Prop`; the model
   front faces Unity +Z.

## Rebuilding

```
blender -b --python scripts/make_atlas.py     # atlases + atlas_uvs.json
blender -b --python scripts/build_all.py      # .blend files + FBX + build_stats.json
blender -b --python scripts/verify.py         # re-imports every FBX and checks it
blender -b --python scripts/make_review.py    # review sheets
blender -b --python scripts/make_manifest.py  # this file
```
